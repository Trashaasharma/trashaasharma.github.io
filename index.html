Superstore sales: growth drivers and a tested 2019 forecast

Is growth coming from more orders, bigger orders or new customers, and how far can a forecast be trusted?

  • Growth came from repeat orders: orders rose 75% from 2015 to 2018 while average order value fell 14%.
  • New customers dried up: first-time buyers fell from 141 in 2016 to 11 in 2018.
  • September, November and December earn 43% of the year's sales.
  • The 2019 forecast is 830k, 15% above 2018. Tested on 2018 first, it missed by 18.7% a month on average.

Practice dataset. Sales only: the file has no profit or cost data.

Store sales: where revenue comes from, and whether discounts work

Which products and customers drive revenue, and do loyalty discounts raise how much people spend?

  • Electronics is 10% of orders but 63% of revenue.
  • Discounts rise with loyalty, not with spending: average order value is 285, 283 and 291 across the three discount bands.
  • A large gender gap in spend is a product-mix effect: within the categories both groups buy, averages are within 5 of each other.

Practice dataset of 5,000 orders. Revenue only: the file has no cost, date or currency.

What I use

Excel

KPI cards, slicers and charts built in the Power BI service, with DAX measures for order counts, averages and growth.
Python
used to cross-check the SQL and Excel results and to compare the forecast with Holt-Winters.
Forecasting
A seasonal index with a straight-line trend, tested on a held-out year and compared with Holt-Winters before it is used.
Reporting
Findings written as one-line answers, with the limits stated next to them: sales are not profit, and a forecast is not a promise.
